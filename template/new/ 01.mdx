Homepage Messaging Guide & Playbook

This playbook is a long-term reference for writing, reviewing, and evolving your website homepage. Its purpose is to ensure your messaging always stays clear, non-technical, customer-centric, and conversion-focused, regardless of future changes in tools, features, or technology.

This is not copywriting only. It is a thinking framework you can return to months or years later.


---

1. The One Rule That Governs Everything

> Never explain automation.



Your homepage must never try to teach:

What automation is

How AI works

How workflows are built

What tools are involved


Why? Because your customer:

Does not understand these concepts

Does not want to learn them

Does not need them to buy


Instead, your homepage exists to create recognition and relief.


---

2. The Core Belief You Must Install in the Reader

Every section of the homepage should reinforce one belief:

> If work follows rules and repeats, it should not be done by a human.



This belief replaces the question:

> “Can this be automated?”



With the realization:

> “Why is a person still doing this?”



If the reader leaves your homepage believing this, your job is done.


---

3. How Your Customer Thinks (Important)

Your customer does not think in terms of:

Tasks

Processes

Systems

Automation


They think in terms of:

“This takes too much time”

“We do this every day”

“Someone always has to check this”

“If we forget, things break”


Your language must mirror how work feels, not how it is designed.


---

4. What You Are Really Selling

You are not selling software. You are not selling AI. You are not selling workflows.

You are selling:

> Freedom from manual execution



Another way to phrase it:

> “We remove work that doesn’t require human judgment.”



This is the lens you must use when writing any line of copy.


---

5. Homepage Structure (Conceptual, Not Tactical)

Your homepage should read like a clear explanation of reality, not a sales pitch.

It must do four things in order:

1. Challenge how they think about work


2. Normalize their frustration


3. Remove responsibility from them


4. Offer a simple next step




---

6. Section-by-Section Playbook

Section 1: Hero (Immediate Reframing)

Goal: Stop them and reset their thinking.

Must Do:

Make a bold but calm statement

Avoid all technical terms

Speak as a fact, not a promise


Correct Tone: Confident, obvious, almost boring

Example Direction (not mandatory wording):

“You shouldn’t be doing repetitive work.”

“Most business work should run without human involvement.”


CTA at this stage should feel low-pressure:

“Explain how your work currently happens”



---

Section 2: The Insight (Why This Happens)

Goal: Make them feel understood.

Explain that businesses are busy not because work is complex, but because:

The same steps repeat

Decisions follow rules

Humans are used for execution


Key idea to communicate:

> “Repetition is not a staffing problem. It’s a system problem.”



Avoid examples. Avoid lists. Speak in concepts.


---

Section 3: The Cost of Manual Work (Emotional Weight)

Goal: Create urgency without fear.

Communicate that manual work creates:

Delays

Errors

Dependence on individuals

Constant checking and follow-ups


Important framing:

> “Most companies accept this as normal. It isn’t.”



This reframes their current state as fixable, not inevitable.


---

Section 4: Authority Transfer (This Is Critical)

Goal: Remove the need for them to decide anything technical.

You must clearly say:

> “You don’t need to know what can be automated.”



Then immediately follow with:

> “You explain how work happens today. We redesign it so it runs automatically.”



This section builds trust by:

Taking responsibility

Demonstrating confidence

Reducing mental effort



---

Section 5: How It Works (Extreme Simplicity)

Goal: Reduce complexity anxiety.

Limit this to three steps maximum:

1. You describe the work


2. We build and operate the system


3. You monitor results



Avoid:

Tools

Platforms

Technical verbs


Use human language only.


---

Section 6: What Makes This Different

Goal: Position without comparison tables.

Key message:

> “This is not a tool you have to learn.”



Reinforce:

Fully managed

No setup

No internal resources needed


This section answers the silent fear:

> “Will this become another thing to manage?”



Your answer must be clearly: No.


---

Section 7: Ongoing Confidence & Safety

Goal: Eliminate long-term risk concerns.

Communicate that:

Systems are hosted by you

Performance is monitored

Issues are handled without escalation


Important idea:

> “Once it runs, it stays running.”




---

Section 8: Final CTA (Relief, Not Action)

Goal: Make the next step feel like relief.

Avoid aggressive CTAs.

Good directional framing:

“Stop doing work that doesn’t require a human.”

“Tell us what you want to stop doing manually.”


The CTA should feel like unloading a burden, not starting a project.


---

7. Language Rules (Non-Negotiable)

Always avoid:

Automation jargon

Tool names

Technical explanations

Buzzwords


Always prefer:

Plain language

Declarative statements

Calm authority

Simple sentences


If a sentence sounds impressive to engineers, it is wrong for the homepage.


---

8. How to Check If Your Homepage Is Correct

Ask these questions:

1. Can a non-technical business owner understand everything?


2. Does the page remove responsibility from the reader?


3. Does it create relief instead of excitement?


4. Does it avoid teaching how things work?



If yes to all four — the homepage is doing its job.


---

9. Final Principle (Remember This)

Your homepage is not there to show what you can automate.

It is there to make the reader realize:

> “I should not be doing this work anymore.”



Once they feel that, they will come to you with clarity — even if they cannot name the problem.
