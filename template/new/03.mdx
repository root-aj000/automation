It is completely normal to feel blank right now. You have done the hard strategic work (defining the problem, the constraints, and the philosophy), and you have the assets (template, frameworks). Now you are standing at the "bridge" between **Strategy** and **Execution**, and you don't know how to cross it without making a mess.

The mistake most people make is pasting *everything* (strategy, template, frameworks, questions) into the chat at once. That confuses the LLM and produces generic results.

To get the best copy without overwhelming the AI (or yourself), you need to follow a **3-Step "Layering" Process.**

Here is exactly what you should ask next, step-by-step.

### Step 1: Prime the "Brain" (The Context Layer)
First, you need to teach the AI *how* to think before you ask it to write a single word. The summary you provided above (Points 1-6) is actually the perfect instruction manual.

**Copy and paste this prompt first:**

> "I am going to help you understand my business strategy. Please read the following 6 points carefully. Do not write any copy yet. Just acknowledge that you understand my constraints, specifically that I need to sell automation to non-technical people without using use cases, technical jargon, or asking them to define the workflow. Here is the strategy: [PASTE YOUR POINTS 1-6 HERE]"

### Step 2: Define the "Container" (The Structure Layer)
Now that the AI understands the *philosophy*, you need to show it the *shape* of the website. You mentioned you have a pre-existing template.

**Copy and paste this prompt second:**

> "Great. Now I want to show you the structure of my homepage. I have a specific template I need to fill. Please analyze this structure. Again, do not write the copy yet, just tell me you understand the flow of the page. Here is the template structure: [PASTE YOUR HOMEPAGE TEMPLATE/WIREFRAME HERE - e.g., Hero Section, Sub-headline, 3 Value Columns, CTA]"

### Step 3: The Execution (The Writing Layer)
Now comes the magic. Instead of asking for the whole page at once, **do it section by section.** This allows you to inject those "frameworks and questions" you mentioned exactly where they belong.

**For the Hero Section (Example):**
> "Now, let's write the **Hero Section** (Headline + Subheadline + CTA).
>
> **Goal:** Based on the strategy in Step 1, I need a headline that makes the user realize they are doing unnecessary manual work, without mentioning 'automation' or technical terms.
>
> **Constraint:** It must be punchy and universal.
>
> **My Frameworks:** I have a list of frameworks, and for this section, I want to use [INSERT ONE OF YOUR FRAMEWORKS HERE, e.g., 'The Old Way vs The New Way'].
>
> Please give me 5 distinct options for the Headline and Subheadline."

---

### Why this approach works:
1.  **You separate Logic from Creativity:** By doing Step 1 separately, you ensure the AI never slips back into using technical jargon. You’ve set the "rules of the game."
2.  **You avoid Token Overload:** By feeding the template separately, the AI focuses on structure.
3.  **You maintain control:** By writing section-by-section (Step 3), you can look at the output and say, "That's too technical, try again," or "That's great, move to the next section."

**Your Next Move:**
Ignore your list of frameworks/questions for a moment. Just do **Step 1** (The Prompt above). Paste your 6-point summary into the chat and let the AI digest that first. Then move to the template.
